Knowledge hub

What I read, listen to and learn from

The books, podcasts, people and platforms that shape how I analyse data and communicate insight.

  • Data Visualization

    Storytelling with Data

    Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic

    Takeaway

    Good analysis is not enough; the insight must also be communicated clearly.

    Knaflic shows how to turn analysis into a message an audience actually understands. She covers choosing the right chart for the question, stripping out clutter that competes for attention, and using colour and position to direct the eye to what matters. For my Power BI work, the lesson is to start from the one insight a stakeholder needs and design the visual around it.

  • Self-Development

    Emotional Intelligence

    Daniel Goleman

    Takeaway

    Professional effectiveness depends not only on technical ability but also on understanding and managing emotions.

    Goleman argues that self-awareness, self-management, motivation, empathy and relationship skills shape success as much as intellect does. For an analyst, these skills decide whether findings land: reading a stakeholder's concerns, staying composed when numbers are challenged, and delivering difficult news constructively. The book reframes communication, teamwork and leadership as abilities that can be practised and improved, not fixed personality traits.

  • Data Science / Analytics

    Data Science for Business

    Foster Provost & Tom Fawcett

    Takeaway

    The purpose of data science is not simply to build models; it is to solve meaningful business problems.

    Provost and Fawcett explain core data science ideas through business problems rather than code: framing a question as a prediction task, building and evaluating models, avoiding overfitting, and judging results by the value they create. It provides a shared vocabulary for working with data scientists and a useful discipline: ask which decision a model will improve before building anything.

  • Decision Making

    Thinking, Fast and Slow

    Daniel Kahneman

    Takeaway

    Better decisions require recognizing when intuition may be misleading and when deliberate analysis is necessary.

    Kahneman describes two modes of thinking: a fast, intuitive system that jumps to conclusions, and a slower, deliberate one that checks the work. Through biases such as anchoring, availability and overconfidence, he shows how easily judgment drifts. For anyone presenting data, it is a reminder that good reporting should slow people down at the right moments and make the evidence hard to ignore.

  • Leadership / Purpose

    Start with Why

    Simon Sinek

    Takeaway

    People connect more strongly with a clear purpose than with a list of features or actions.

    Sinek's Golden Circle argues that leaders and organisations inspire action when they communicate why they do something before explaining how and what. Starting with purpose builds trust and lasting loyalty rather than one-off compliance. Applied to analytics, it means clarifying the decision and the purpose behind a reporting request before discussing metrics, tools or visuals.

  • Productivity / Self-Development

    Atomic Habits

    James Clear

    Takeaway

    Small improvements repeated consistently can produce significant long-term change.

    Clear explains how small, repeatable improvements compound into major results. He focuses on building systems instead of chasing goals, tying habits to the identity you want to build, and designing your environment so good behaviours are obvious and easy to repeat. The approach is especially practical for continuous learning, such as building SQL, Power BI and certification skills alongside full-time work.

  • Business Analysis

    A Guide to the Business Analysis Body of Knowledge (BABOK Guide)

    International Institute of Business Analysis (IIBA)

    Takeaway

    Effective business analysis connects business needs with practical, measurable solutions.

    The BABOK Guide is the recognised framework for business analysis practice. It organises the work into knowledge areas: elicitation and collaboration, stakeholder engagement, strategy analysis, requirements analysis and design, and solution evaluation. For a modern analyst working between business teams, data, technology and decision-makers, it offers a shared structure for turning vague needs into clear, testable requirements.

  • Personal Development / Finance

    The Psychology of Money

    Morgan Housel

    Takeaway

    Financial decisions are often driven more by behavior and psychology than by mathematical knowledge.

    Housel shows that financial outcomes depend less on technical knowledge than on behaviour: personal experience, emotion, patience and how we perceive risk. Through short, memorable stories he explains compounding, leaving room for error and knowing when enough is enough. Alongside an accounting background, it is a reminder that people rarely act on numbers alone, which matters whenever analysis meets real decisions.

  • Data Analytics / Career

    How to Become a Data Analyst

    Annie Nelson

    Takeaway

    Becoming a strong data analyst requires combining technical skills with the ability to understand and solve business problems.

    Drawing on her own career change, Nelson lays out a practical, low-cost roadmap into data analytics: the core tools such as SQL, spreadsheets and visualisation software, building a portfolio, networking, and navigating the job search. Her emphasis on pairing technical skills with business understanding reflects how I see the analyst role: the value lies in solving real problems, not simply in using tools.

  • Self-Development / Leadership

    The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People

    Stephen R. Covey

    Takeaway

    Long-term effectiveness comes from principles, priorities, relationships, and consistent personal discipline.

    Covey presents a principle-based framework for personal and interpersonal effectiveness: be proactive, begin with the end in mind, put first things first, think win-win, seek first to understand, synergise, and keep renewing yourself. The habits translate directly to analytics work, from prioritising the requests that matter most to listening carefully before proposing a dashboard or solution.
