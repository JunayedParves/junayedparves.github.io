Nexora Apparels: 20-Year Supply Chain Intelligence

Twenty years of a Bangladesh garment group told as one story: growth, delivery, cost and productivity, page by page.

Executive summary

Nexora Apparels is a fictional garment group: 8 companies, 26 factories, 52 buyers and 180 suppliers. I rebuilt its management dashboard around Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic's Storytelling with Data. Every page answers one management question, and every chart title states the answer, recalculated live for whatever is filtered.

Before telling any story, I audited the numbers. The inherited dashboard showed a 100% gross margin because cost of goods was silently blank. Ten measures were wrong and nine charts rendered nothing. A beautiful dashboard built on a wrong margin is worse than no dashboard.

All data is synthetic (≈3.04M rows, calibrated to public BGMEA, Bangladesh Bank and BBS benchmarks). Nexora and its companies are fictional.

Business problem

From the top, Nexora looks like a success: revenue grew 4.0×, from $236M in 2006 to $939M in 2025, at a steady ~50% operational gross margin. Leadership needed to know whether that headline hid problems in delivery, cost and productivity, and where to act first.

Analytical objectives

  • Audit and correct the semantic model so every number reconciles to the cost ledger.
  • Answer one management question per page: growth, market, commercial, procurement, inventory, production, quality and finance.
  • Make each chart state its finding in the title, and keep that sentence true under any filter.
  • Point management to specific levers: companies, buyers, suppliers and cost lines.

Dataset

22 Parquet tables (≈3.04M rows, 2006–2025): 10 conformed dimensions and 12 fact tables, including monthly snapshots for inventory, workforce, ESG and macro indicators. The data is reproducible from a seeded Python generator, and BigQuery DDL is included for a warehouse load.

Data audit & quality fixes

  • Fixed gross margin, which showed 100% because COGS was blank. It now shows 50.2%, reconciled to the cost ledger.
  • Corrected 10 wrong measures and repaired 9 charts that rendered nothing (for example, bars bound to a non-existent role).
  • Made inventory and headcount semi-additive, so stock is never summed across months, and annualised turnover and DIO.
  • Replaced a 7-slice donut and a dual-axis combo with sorted bars and indexed lines, so unlike units share one honest axis.

Data model & storytelling engine

  • Star schema: 23 tables, 64 relationships, 215 measures, with inactive date roles for ship and arrival dates.
  • Storytelling engine: 8 DAX user-defined functions (Nexora.*). Every title, KPI context line and highlight colour is one line of DAX calling a shared function.
  • Colour logic computed by DAX: grey for context, navy for the point, orange for the problem, so the highlight moves with the data.
  • Filter rules: year trends ignore the Year filter and company rankings ignore the Company filter, so a benchmark is always visible.
  • Accessibility: WCAG-checked colours (text ≥ 7:1, marks ≥ 3:1) and alt text on every visual.

The dashboard

Every page uses the same layout: question → scoreboard → answer → evidence. KPI cards show a "2025 vs 2006" context line that turns orange only when the move is bad.

Key findings

  1. Growth hid a delivery crisis. On-time-in-full fell from 63% to 40%, and days of inventory rose from 16 to 56.
  2. Nexora lost share. It grew 4.0× against 4.7× for national RMG exports.
  3. Costs are structural, not seasonal. The minimum wage rose 7.5× while output per labour hour rose 1.1×; payroll grew 9.5× on 1.8× headcount.
  4. Import dependence has not moved in 20 years. 76% of materials are imported, 40% from China, and customs and inland congestion cause 40% of late shipment volume.
  5. Two buyers generate 41% of revenue, and traditional EU/UK/US markets still supply 92%.
  6. Specific companies are the levers. Dhaleswari trails on margin (26.6% vs 50.2%) and line efficiency (74.8% vs 87.6%); Surma has the highest defect rate (4.9% vs 3.7%).
  7. Procurement is the biggest margin lever: direct material is 78% of total cost. Carbon per garment fell 33% as solar reached about 20%.

Decisions the dashboard supports

The big idea: Nexora won the volume game. The next decade's margin will be won in the supply chain: delivery reliability, import dependence and productivity per worker. Each page ends in a decision:

  • Where leadership attention goes this quarter: growth, or delivery recovery.
  • Whether productivity investment (automation, lean lines) is now overdue, starting with Dhaleswari.
  • Dual-sourcing and backward-linkage targets to cut lead time and import dependence.
  • A DIO target and a raw-material reorder policy.
  • Buyer-concentration risk appetite, and whether to court non-traditional markets.

Technical implementation

  • Power BI Project (PBIP) with a TMDL model and PBIR report, so every page, visual and measure is a reviewable text file.
  • Report pages, storytelling measures and interactions generated and validated by Node.js scripts.
  • Synthetic data generator in Python (pandas, pyarrow, DuckDB) with a fixed seed; BigQuery DDL for warehouse loading.
  • Requires a 2025+ Power BI Desktop build (DAX user-defined functions, compatibility level 1702).

Limitations & assumptions

  • Synthetic data calibrated to public benchmarks: patterns are realistic, but no real company is described.
  • The dashboard is descriptive and diagnostic. It sizes where to look; it does not forecast or optimise.
  • Comparisons with national RMG exports use public aggregate benchmarks.
