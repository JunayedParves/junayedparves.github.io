Databel: Customer Churn Review

A four-page executive Power BI report on telecom churn: who leaves, why, and the three changes most likely to keep them.

Executive summary

Databel, a telecom provider, loses 1 in 4 customers (26.9%, 1,796 of 6,687), and with them $66.1K of $207.5K in monthly revenue (31.9%). I built a four-page executive Power BI report that explains who churns and why, and ends with three recommendations.

Because not every reviewer has Power BI, I also published an interactive browser replica of all four pages with the same filters, computed from the same CSV. A pandas script re-checks every validation figure against the source.

Databel is a fictional company in a practice dataset. The findings describe that dataset, not a real operator.

Business problem

Losing a quarter of customers, and almost a third of monthly revenue, is unsustainable. Management needed to know which customers leave, why they leave, and which levers (contracts, support, plan design) would reduce churn.

Analytical objectives

  • Quantify churn and the revenue lost with it.
  • Rank the reasons customers give for leaving.
  • Test how contract type, tenure, support calls, plan fit and demographics relate to churn.
  • Translate the strongest signals into a short list of recommendations.

Dataset

One customer-level table: 6,687 customers and 29 columns including contract type, tenure, payment method, plans and add-ons, support calls, demographics, state, churn label, churn category and churn reason.

Data preparation & validation

  • Converted empty Churn Category and Churn Reason text into true nulls in Power Query, so blank filtering and reason counts work, with displayed values unchanged.
  • Held the data path in a DataFolder parameter so the project refreshes on any machine.
  • validate.py rebuilds the model logic in pandas and checks every figure: totals, contract, tenure, payment, service calls, plan fit, age and senior × contract.
  • validate_report.py parses every report JSON file, checks field references and runs Microsoft's PBIR validator.

Approach

  • Churn rate and revenue-lost measures in DAX, with headline sentences bound to measures so they update with slicers.
  • Segment comparisons: contract × tenure, support calls, international-plan fit, group versus individual, age and senior × contract.
  • A Top 10 states table with in-cell data bars instead of a filled map, which current Power BI Desktop blocks and which would send state names to an online service.
  • The whole PBIP project is generated from a Python script, so every page, visual and measure is reproducible.

Key metrics

MetricDefinition
Churn rateChurned customers ÷ all customers
Revenue lostMonthly charges of churned customers
Churn by segmentChurn rate within each contract, tenure, plan or demographic group

The report

Four pages with slicers for state, age group, contract type and gender. The live web version reproduces all of them in the browser.

Key findings

  1. Competitors are the main reason: 45.5% of churners with a recorded reason left for a competitor ("better offer" 303, "better devices" 297).
  2. Contract type is the strongest signal: month-to-month customers churn at 46.3%, against 11.3% on one-year and 2.8% on two-year contracts (about 17×). Seniors on month-to-month contracts churn at 74.6%.
  3. New customers leave fastest: churn is 53.1% in the first 6 months and falls to 9.7% after 4 years.
  4. The third support call is a tipping point: churn jumps from 36.6% at 2 calls to 87.5% at 3, and 99.7–100% at 4 or more.
  5. Plan fit matters: customers paying for the international plan without calling abroad churn at 71.2%, against 7.6% for those who use it; group-plan customers churn at 6.5% versus 32.8% for individuals.
  6. California is an outlier at 63.2% churn, though on only 68 customers.

Recommendations

  • Move month-to-month customers onto 1–2 year contracts. They churn at 46%, compared with 3% on two-year plans.
  • Escalate any customer on their third support call. 88–100% of them leave.
  • Fix plan mismatch. Sell the international plan to customers who actually call abroad, and push group plans, which churn at 7%.

Technical implementation

  • Power BI Project (PBIP) with TMDL model and PBIR report, generated by _build/generate_pbip.py.
  • Custom theme with a content-hashed file name so theme edits reload in Desktop.
  • Interactive web replica on GitHub Pages, rebuilt by build_web.py from the same CSV.

Limitations & assumptions

  • These are associations in observational data, not proof of cause.
  • Some segments are small (California has 68 customers), so their rates are less stable.
  • Churn reasons exist only for churned customers, so they explain why leavers left, not why others stayed.
