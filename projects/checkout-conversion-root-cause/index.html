Project Tollgate: Checkout Conversion Root Cause

An Excel root cause analysis that tested every explanation for a sudden checkout drop until one was left, and costed it.

Executive summary

Checkout completion fell sharply in June 2026 and revenue fell with it. The product team suspected an ongoing PayPal integration issue. I built a single Excel workbook that answers four questions in order: is the data clean enough to trust, when did the decline start, which segments cause it, and how much revenue it has cost.

The answer: PayQuick, a new payment option, is the cause. PayPal is not. PayQuick went live at 00:03 on 18 June, the same minute the decline began, and 95% of PayQuick checkouts time out at order authorisation.

The dataset is a practice case dataset of a fictional online store. Every figure in the workbook is a live formula, cross-checked by a separate Python analysis.

Business problem

A drop in checkout completion is expensive every day it continues, and fixing the wrong cause wastes engineering time. The business needed a defensible root cause, a cost estimate and a plan, quickly.

Analytical objectives

  • Confirm the data is trustworthy before analysing it.
  • Find the break date statistically rather than by eye.
  • Test every plausible hypothesis (PayPal, device or browser, traffic mix, a coupon campaign, the new payment option) and rule each in or out.
  • Estimate the revenue lost, and the cost of every further day without a fix.

Dataset

26,375 checkout sessions from June 2026, with payment method, device, OS, browser, region, traffic source, coupon, checkout stage reached, error codes and order value.

Data cleaning & validation

The 1_Cleaning sheet runs 13 live data-quality checks before any analysis: duplicates, blanks, invalid values, contradictions between fields and outliers. The cleaned sessions sit in an Excel table that every other sheet reads from.

Analytical methodology

  • Break date: daily metrics plus a scan of every possible break date using a two-proportion z-test; the date is selected by formula (INDEX/MATCH on the z-score scan).
  • Segments: before-versus-after completion for 30 segments with z-tests, the result with PayQuick removed, and where each payment method's sessions stop in checkout.
  • Revenue impact: three independent methods (session-level estimate, top-down and outlier-capped) plus a revenue bridge.
  • Cross-check: tollgate_rca.py repeats the whole analysis in Python; every Excel number matches.

The workbook

Seven sheets: Dashboard, Executive Summary, 1_Cleaning, 2_Break_Date, 3_Segments, 4_Revenue and Data. Chart titles state their conclusion, and summary sentences are built with TEXT() so they update with the data.

Key findings

  1. The decline began at 00:03 on 18 June, the minute PayQuick went live.
  2. 95% of PayQuick checkouts time out at order authorisation (PAYMENT_AUTH_TIMEOUT), and every timeout is a PayQuick session.
  3. Completion fell from 68.7% to 59.1%. With PayQuick sessions excluded it is 68.8%, unchanged.
  4. PayPal was ruled out: 69.6% → 68.8%, not significant (p = 0.45), with zero timeouts.
  5. Estimated loss: $70,943 in 13 days, about $164K every 30 days if unfixed.

Recommendations

  • Hide PayQuick at checkout today.
  • Fix the authorisation timeout with the vendor.
  • Relaunch to 5% of traffic first, with alerts on completion by payment method.
  • Email the 1,595 customers it turned away ($104K in carts).

Technical implementation

  • COUNTIFS and SUMIFS for every metric, driven by a single break-date cell.
  • Two-proportion z-tests with SQRT and NORMSDIST; SUMPRODUCT for consistency checks and the outlier-capped estimate.
  • MINIFS, MAXIFS, PERCENTILE and QUARTILE for outlier detection.
  • Native charts, including a waterfall built from stacked columns; a colour convention for inputs, links and formulas.
  • Python (pandas, NumPy, openpyxl) to clean the data, cross-check the analysis and build the workbook.

Limitations & assumptions

  • The data is a practice case, not a real store's sessions.
  • Revenue loss is an estimate; three methods are shown so the range is visible.
  • Statistical tests show association in observational data; the timing, error codes and removal test together make the causal case.
